Part 2 — Team Agreement
1. Team Values
We commit to: respect (listening to every member's input before deciding),
accountability (owning our individually assigned work), reliability (showing up prepared
to meetings and hitting agreed deadlines), and transparency (raising problems early
instead of letting them surface at the deadline).

2. Meetings
● We will meet once a week: a 30-minute sync after lab, via Zoom/Discord or in
person at the library.
● If someone can't attend, they must notify the team at least 4 hours in advance
and review the meeting notes/recording afterward.
● Two consecutive unexcused absences trigger a check-in conversation with the
team, per the Accountability section below.

3. Communication
● Primary channel: Discord for day-to-day discussion and file sharing.
● Urgent matters: text message or phone call, not just a channel post.
● Milestone decisions, deadlines, and task assignments are posted in a pinned
channel or shared doc so nothing lives only in someone's DMs.

4. Response Expectations
● Team members respond to messages within 24 hours on weekdays, 48 hours on
weekends.
● If someone can't respond in time (exams, personal emergency), they post a
heads up as soon as they're able, even briefly.
● If a member misses this window repeatedly (3+ times) without communicating,
another member follows up directly, and if it continues, it's raised as a team issue
per the Accountability section.

5. Respect & Professional Conduct
● Respectful behavior means: critiquing code/ideas, not people; assuming good
faith; giving everyone a turn to speak in meetings before moving to a decision.
● Unacceptable: dismissing someone's contribution without explanation, talking
over people repeatedly, working around a member instead of with them.
● We'll rotate who leads each meeting so facilitation (and airtime) doesn't default to
the same one or two people.

6. Accountability
If a member doesn't meet an agreed expectation (missed task, missed meeting, no
communication), the team first raises it directly and informally. If it happens again, it's
documented in writing (e.g., a shared log) with a specific task and timeline. If it
continues after that, the team escalates to the TA/instructor with that documentation.

7. Decision-Making
● Day-to-day technical decisions: whoever owns that part of the codebase decides,
informed by a quick team check if it affects others.
● Larger decisions (architecture, scope, task division): discussed as a team,
decided by majority vote if consensus isn't reached within one meeting.
● If a vote ties or a disagreement is technical, we timebox research (e.g., 1 day)
and revisit with evidence rather than debating opinions indefinitely.

8. Conflict Resolution
● The two people involved talk directly and try to resolve it first.
● If unresolved, it's brought to the full team in a scheduled meeting (not over text)
to discuss calmly.
● If still unresolved after that, the team brings it to the TA or instructor together, not
as a one-sided complaint.

9. Anticipated Challenges
● Missed meetings: rotating meeting times slightly to accommodate different
schedules; sharing notes so absence doesn't mean being out of the loop.
● Missed deadlines: breaking milestone work into smaller sub-deadlines with buffer
before the real one.
● Unequal contributions: tracking tasks/PRs in a shared board so contribution is
visible early, not just at the end.
● Poor communication: the 24-hour response norm above, enforced consistently.
● Scheduling conflicts: collecting everyone's weekly availability upfront and picking
meeting slots that actually work for all four.
● Disagreements: the conflict resolution steps above, used before frustration builds
up.
